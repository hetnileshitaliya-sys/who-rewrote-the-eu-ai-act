Who rewrote the EU AI Act?

Research question: whose demands did the final Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, match: industry, public-interest groups or public authorities?

An evidence map of written feedback to the European Commission's proposal COM(2025)836 on the Have Your Say portal (21 November 2025 to 13 March 2026), compared article by article with the law that entered into force on 27 July 2026. 80 of the 274 submissions are coded: every organisation in the smaller actor groups plus a random draw from the larger ones. Individual citizens are excluded and not named. Codes follow codebook.md in the project repository.

Het Italiya, University of Bristol. Independent project. Last updated __DATE__.

Definitions and method

Position. For each of eight contested issues, a submission is coded as opposes the Commission's change, supports it as drafted, wants it to go further, or not mentioned. Every code carries a short quote from the submission.

Match with the final law. Each position is compared with what Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 actually did, checked against the official texts on EUR-Lex. A demand is won if the final law matches it, partly won if the law moved in its direction but less far, and lost if the law went the other way. The share shown counts won as 1 and partly won as 0.5.

What this shows and does not show. It shows correspondence between stated positions and the final text. It does not show influence: the law also moved through Parliament, Council and lobbying this map cannot see.

Actor groups. Submitters keep the type they registered, and are grouped as industry (companies and business associations), public-interest and independent (NGOs, unions, consumer groups, academics) or public authorities. Five organisations whose self-registered type did not match what they are were regrouped, and each is flagged in the table. Three "other" submitters stay in the table but are left out of the charts.

Limitations

    __VERIFY__
  • Many submissions addressed the GDPR, Data Act or cyber rules in the same package. Only positions on the eight AI Act issues are coded, so most organisations are "not mentioned" on most issues.
  • One submission's PDF had no extractable text and could not be coded.
  • Several judgement calls are recorded case by case, for example where a group asked for more delay than the law gave. The outcome note says why.
