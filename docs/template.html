Who rewrote the EU AI Act?

Whose demands did the final law, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, match: industry, public-interest groups, public authorities or citizens?

Every unique written submission to the Commission's proposal COM(2025)836 on the Have Your Say portal (21 November 2025 to 13 March 2026) is coded on eight contested issues, then tracked through Parliament and Council to the text that entered into force on 27 July 2026.

Method

How the coding works

Who is counted. All 274 portal submissions, with repeat filings by the same organisation reduced to the latest one: 270 unique submissions, of which 154 take a position on at least one of the eight issues. The other 116 addressed the GDPR, Data Act or cyber rules in the same package, or nothing the codebook covers. Individual citizens are included as a group and never named.

Position. For each issue a submission is coded as opposing the Commission's change, supporting it as drafted, wanting it to go further, or not mentioning it. Every code carries a short quote from the submission, in its original language where it was not written in English.

Outcome. Each position is scored against what Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 did, checked on EUR-Lex. Won if the final law matches it, partly won if the law moved its way but less far, lost if it went the other way. Where a demand differed from what the law did (for example a 24-month delay when the law set fixed dates), the outcome was judged by hand and the reason is recorded next to it.

Groups. Submitters keep the type they registered on the portal, grouped as industry, public-interest and independent (NGOs, unions, consumer groups, academics), public authorities, individual citizens and other. Ten organisations whose registered type did not match what they are were regrouped; each is flagged in the appendix.

What this shows and does not show. It shows how closely stated positions correspond to the final text. It does not show influence. The law also moved through Parliament and Council negotiations, and through lobbying this map cannot see.

Limitations

What to keep in mind

    __VERIFY__
  • Coding works from keyword-located passages, followed by a second, broader search over every cell left blank. A position stated only in passing, or in a language the search terms miss, can still be missed.
  • Two submissions (European AI Forum and the German AI Association) filed near-identical texts. Both are counted, as the portal lists them separately.
  • Council's position on moving machinery out of Section A is taken from reporting on the negotiations, not from the Council's own published text.
  • The 80-submission sample published earlier gave industry 57% and public-interest groups 43%. The full census gives 57% and 42%, so the sample estimate held.
